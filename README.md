Rizal in Belgium: A Journey with José Rizal

A single-page, interactive travel brochure about Dr. José Rizal's stay in Belgium (Brussels and Ghent, 1890 to 1891). It was made for the Chapter 9 activity, "A Journey Through Rizal's Travels."

Files

How to view
Double-click index.html to open it in a modern browser (Chrome, Edge or Firefox).
An internet connection is needed the first time, because the fonts (Fraunces, Inter and Caveat) load from Google Fonts.

No installation, build step or server is required.

Sections
Hero: Animated title, a route from Paris to Brussels to Ghent, and a passport with dated stamps.
Welcome to Belgium: A short introduction to the country and its history.
Travel diary: Six stops (leaving Paris, the Brussels room, bad news from Calamba, the return to Brussels, Ghent, and the printing of El Filibusterismo), shown as horizontal scrolling cards.
Important places: A simplified map with clickable pins for the Brussels boarding house, Ghent, and the print shop.
Important people: The Jacoby sisters, José Alejandrino, Valentin Ventura, Paciano Rizal, Ferdinand Blumentritt, and José Basa with Sixto Lopez.
Ideas and observations: How the events of 1890 and 1891 shaped the novel.
Timeline: Key dates, drawn as you scroll.
Interesting facts and lessons: Flip cards and three lessons from Rizal's journey.
Sources: References for the content.
Features
Scroll-triggered animations and a progress bar
Horizontal scrolling diary with a route indicator
Clickable map pins and place tabs (keyboard accessible)
Flip cards that work with hover, tap or keyboard
Responsive layout with a mobile menu
Respects the "reduce motion" system setting
Customizing
Text: Search for the section comments such as <!-- PANEL 2: JOURNEY --> and edit the text beneath them.
Colors: Change the variables at the top of the <style> block (--night, --gold, --red, --paper).
Sources: Replace the placeholder entry "Course textbook…" in the Sources section with your textbook's full citation, if one is required.
Accuracy notes
Dates and events were checked against the online sources listed on the page. Compare them with your course textbook before submitting.
The map is simplified and not drawn to scale.
Historians disagree about the identity of "Petite Suzanne," the young woman in the Brussels boarding house. The site presents this as an open question.
